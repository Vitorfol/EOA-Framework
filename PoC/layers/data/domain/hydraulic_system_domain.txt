# Hydraulic Systems Condition Monitoring - Domain Knowledge

## Overview

This dataset contains condition monitoring data from a hydraulic test rig, designed to simulate and detect various failure modes and degradation states in industrial hydraulic systems. The test rig is representative of real-world applications in manufacturing, construction equipment, and heavy machinery.

## System Description

### Hydraulic Test Rig Components

The test rig consists of the following primary components:

1. **Primary Working Circuit**
   - Pump: Provides hydraulic power to the system
   - Motor: Drives the hydraulic pump
   - Hydraulic accumulator: Stores pressurized hydraulic fluid
   - Proportional valve: Controls flow and pressure

2. **Cooling System**
   - Cooler: Maintains optimal operating temperature
   - Cooling efficiency sensors (virtual): Monitor cooling performance

3. **Distribution System**
   - Multiple pressure points (PS1-PS6): Monitor pressure throughout the system
   - Flow sensors (FS1-FS2): Monitor volume flow rates
   - Temperature sensors (TS1-TS4): Monitor thermal conditions

## Monitored Variables

### Sensor Types and Sampling Frequencies

The system employs 17 sensors with three different sampling frequencies:

#### High Frequency Sensors (100 Hz)
- **PS1-PS6 (Pressure Sensors)**: Measure pressure at different points in the hydraulic circuit
  - Critical for detecting leaks, valve issues, and accumulator problems
  - High frequency captures rapid pressure fluctuations
  
- **EPS1 (Motor Power)**: Monitors electrical power consumption
  - Indicates pump efficiency and mechanical load

#### Medium Frequency Sensors (10 Hz)
- **FS1 (Volume Flow - Pump)**: Measures flow rate from the pump
- **FS2 (Volume Flow - Cooler)**: Measures flow rate through the cooler
  - Flow sensors detect leakage and valve degradation
  - Medium frequency sufficient for flow dynamics

#### Low Frequency Sensors (1 Hz)
- **TS1-TS4 (Temperature Sensors)**: Monitor thermal conditions at key points
  - Thermal changes occur slowly, requiring lower sampling frequency
  - Critical for detecting cooler degradation
  
- **VS1 (Vibration Sensor)**: Monitors system vibration
  - Indicates mechanical wear and instability

- **CE (Cooling Efficiency - Virtual)**: Computed efficiency metric
- **CP (Cooling Power - Virtual)**: Computed power metric  
- **SE (System Efficiency - Virtual)**: Overall efficiency metric

### Measurement Cycles

- Each measurement cycle lasts **60 seconds**
- Total dataset: **2,205 cycles** representing different operating conditions
- Conditions vary between cycles but remain constant within each cycle

## Target Variables (Condition States)

The dataset includes 5 target variables representing different component conditions:

### 1. Cooler Condition
**Values**: 3, 20, 100 (percentage of optimal efficiency)
- **3%**: Close to total failure - severe degradation
- **20%**: Reduced efficiency - moderate degradation
- **100%**: Full efficiency - optimal condition

**Indicators**: 
- High temperatures (TS sensors)
- Poor cooling efficiency (CE, CP)
- Temperature differentials

### 2. Valve Condition  
**Values**: 73, 80, 90, 100 (percentage of optimal switching)
- **73%**: Severe lag in switching behavior
- **80%**: Moderate lag
- **90%**: Slight lag
- **100%**: Optimal switching behavior

**Indicators**:
- Pressure fluctuations (PS sensors)
- Flow irregularities (FS sensors)
- Valve response times

### 3. Pump Leakage
**Values**: 0, 1, 2 (severity level)
- **0**: No leakage
- **1**: Weak leakage
- **2**: Severe leakage

**Indicators**:
- Pressure drops (PS sensors)
- Flow rate reductions (FS sensors)
- Power consumption changes (EPS1)
- Temperature increases (TS sensors)

### 4. Accumulator Pressure
**Values**: 90, 100, 115, 130 (bar)
- **90 bar**: Reduced pressure - below optimal
- **100 bar**: Slightly reduced pressure
- **115 bar**: Optimal pressure
- **130 bar**: Near maximum permissible pressure

**Indicators**:
- Pressure sensor readings (especially PS1, PS2)
- System stability
- Pressure fluctuation patterns

### 5. Stable Flag
**Values**: 0, 1 (binary indicator)
- **0**: Unstable operation - conditions did not reach steady state
- **1**: Stable operation - system reached stable operating regime

**Indicators**:
- Consistency of sensor readings
- Absence of oscillations
- Steady-state achievement

## Failure Modes and Physics

### Cooler Degradation
**Physics**: Reduced heat transfer due to fouling, scaling, or reduced coolant flow
**Effects**:
- Gradual temperature increase throughout system
- Reduced cooling power and efficiency
- Potential thermal stress on components

### Valve Degradation  
**Physics**: Mechanical wear, contamination, or seal degradation
**Effects**:
- Delayed response to control signals
- Pressure oscillations
- Flow irregularities
- Energy losses

### Pump Leakage
**Physics**: Seal wear, mechanical damage, or gap formation
**Effects**:
- Internal fluid bypass
- Reduced volumetric efficiency
- Pressure drops
- Flow rate reductions
- Compensatory power increase
- Heat generation

### Accumulator Issues
**Physics**: Gas pre-charge changes, bladder/membrane degradation
**Effects**:
- Altered pressure buffering capacity
- System instability
- Pressure fluctuations
- Potential resonance effects

## Data Characteristics

### Statistical Properties
- **No missing values**: Complete dataset with all sensors operational
- **Class imbalance**: Some conditions more represented than others
  - Accumulator: High imbalance (5.5:1 ratio)
  - Cooler and Valve: Moderate imbalance (2-3:1 ratios)
  - Pump and Stable Flag: Well balanced

### Temporal Patterns
- **Steady-state focus**: Most cycles represent stable operating conditions
- **Transient detection**: Unstable flag captures non-steady-state behavior
- **Multi-frequency**: Different dynamics captured at appropriate frequencies
  - Fast: Pressure dynamics (100 Hz)
  - Medium: Flow dynamics (10 Hz)
  - Slow: Thermal dynamics (1 Hz)

### Correlations
- Strong correlations between pressure sensors (system-wide effects)
- Moderate correlations between flow and pressure (hydraulic relationships)
- Temperature sensors show thermal coupling
- Virtual sensors combine multiple physical measurements

## Machine Learning Considerations

### Multi-Label Classification
All 5 target variables can be predicted simultaneously or independently:
- **Joint modeling**: Capture inter-dependencies between failure modes
- **Independent modeling**: Simpler but may miss interactions
- **Recommended**: Start with single-target models, then explore multi-task learning

### Class Imbalance Strategies
- **Accumulator**: Use SMOTE or class weights (high imbalance)
- **Cooler/Valve**: Use class weights (moderate imbalance)
- **Pump/Stable**: Standard methods (balanced)

### Feature Engineering
- **Statistical aggregation**: Mean, std, min, max, range, quartiles
- **Temporal features**: Trends, slopes, change rates
- **Frequency domain**: FFT features for cyclic patterns (pressure, vibration)
- **Cross-sensor**: Ratios, differences, correlations

### Model Selection
- **Tree-based models**: Handle non-linear relationships well
- **Time series models**: Capture temporal dependencies (LSTM, GRU)
- **Hybrid approaches**: Combine statistical and temporal features

### Validation Strategy
- **Stratified splitting**: Maintain class distributions
- **Cross-validation**: 5-fold recommended for robust evaluation
- **Temporal considerations**: Avoid look-ahead bias if modeling real-time
